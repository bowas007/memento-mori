An idea with two thousand years of history

Memento mori is Latin for "remember that you must die." It sounds dark. It's the opposite: for over two thousand years it has been a tool for living well.

You could leave life right now.
Marcus Aurelius · Meditations
Ancient Rome

The whisper in the parade

Tradition holds that when a Roman general paraded through the city in triumph, a slave stood behind him with a single job: to whisper that he, too, was mortal. At the peak of glory — a reminder that it all ends.

The Stoics

A tool, not a threat

The Stoic philosophers made it a daily practice. Seneca advised ending each day as if life were complete. Epictetus told his students to keep death before their eyes. And Marcus Aurelius — the most powerful man alive — kept writing it in his private journal. Not to be morbid, but because nothing sorts out your priorities faster.

Today

From coin to desktop

Medieval painters put skulls and hourglasses in their art for the same reason. Today, author Ryan Holiday (The Daily Stoic) carries a memento mori medallion in his pocket — his point being that remembering death is what gives your days priority and meaning. This wallpaper is that same old practice, drawn on your screen.

Seeing the weeks you've spent makes the ones that remain feel real. That's the whole trick — and it still works, twenty centuries on.

Questions

Why is it free?

It started as a personal tool, and a reminder like this shouldn't sit behind a paywall. If it ends up meaning something to you, there's a donate button — pay what you want, including nothing.

Which Windows versions are supported?

Windows 10 and Windows 11. Everything the app needs is already part of Windows — there are no extra downloads or frameworks.

Does it run in the background all the time?

No. A standard Windows scheduled task renders your wallpaper every Monday at 08:00 and when you log in. It takes about a second, then nothing is running.

What happens with my date of birth?

It's stored in a small settings file on your own PC and used only to draw the grid. It is never sent anywhere.

How do updates work?

The weekly task also checks for a new version. If there is one, it's downloaded, verified and installed silently. Your settings are never touched.

Can I change how it looks?

Yes — open "Memento Mori Settings" from the Start menu. You can switch between weeks and months, change the shape and size of the squares, pick dark or light, and adjust every color, with a live preview.

Why would I want to be reminded that I'm going to die?

Because it works the other way around: seeing the empty squares makes the current one matter. Memento mori is an old Stoic practice — remember death, and you remember to live.
